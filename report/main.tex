\section*{Abstract}
This report analyses NEA five-minute rainfall records from 2017 to 2024. Wet-
reading frequency ranged from 1.94\% to 3.20\%. Among station-months with at
least 95\% coverage, mean observed monthly rainfall ranged from 31.97 to
419.07~mm and conditional wet intensity from 0.58 to 1.35~mm. No station met
the 90\% annual-coverage rule in all eight years, so annual differences reflect
a changing network. For Task 2, a same-station history GBT was selected on
January--June 2023 and calibrated on July--December. On 6,374,476 valid 2024
prediction times at the 0.5-mm threshold, it achieved PR-AUC 0.6345 and Brier
score 0.01952, compared with 0.0732 and 0.03436 for the climatology baseline.
The model used a deterministic 2\% training sample; the baseline used all
training times. These are retrospective event-time results and do not establish
real-time data availability or performance on future station networks.

\section{Introduction}
The report uses the National Environment Agency's Historical Rainfall across
Singapore collection, timestamped in Singapore Time. The catalog warns that
records may be missing and have not undergone climate-record quality control~\cite{nea}.

Task 1 describes all eight years. Task 2 uses 2017--2022 for training,
January--June 2023 for model selection, July--December for calibration, and
2024 for final evaluation. Task 1's 2024 summaries were not used to select the
forecasting model.

\section{Data and cleaning}
\subsection{Input and timestamp handling}
The frozen manifest records eight annual files, their schemas, row counts and
SHA-256 digests. They contain 48,538,713 rows and 12 columns. The rainfall field
is NEA reading type \texttt{TB1 Rainfall 5 Minute Total F}, in millimetres. The
download time was not recorded. The project's
interval-end convention treats a reading at time $t$ as rainfall accumulated
over $(t-5\text{ min},t]$; this is an analysis assumption, not an NEA-confirmed
timestamp definition.

Early 2017 records systematically end at second 59; later 2017 and 2018--2024
records end at second 00. The same \texttt{:59} convention appears in the
official 2016 dataset, which was not used here~\cite{nea}. In 2017, 3,480,779
timestamps fell on five-minute boundaries and 1,775,327 fell one second before
them. The early \texttt{:59} values were advanced one second for alignment, and
the source timestamps were retained. The publisher does not document the
convention change's meaning, so this is an alignment choice; other off-grid
offsets were rejected.

Validation covered parsing, station IDs, timestamps, nonnegative rainfall,
date/year, reading type and unit. Duplicate station-time keys were resolved
deterministically. Coordinates were used only for the supplementary spatial
summary. Missing readings remain gaps and were not filled with zero.

\subsection{Coverage definitions}
Coverage is the count of retained five-minute readings divided by the expected
slots (288 per calendar day) in the full calendar period, including periods
with no records. Rainfall
occurrence is the share of retained readings greater than zero; missing
readings are excluded from this denominator and are not treated as dry.
Conditional wet intensity is the mean five-minute rainfall total among
positive readings. A
station-month rainfall total is the sum of observed readings and is not
adjusted for missing slots.

Monthly summaries include station-months with at least 95\% coverage and give
each eligible station-month equal weight. For hourly summaries, the same
monthly rule applies, and each station-month-hour must also have at least 95\%
of its expected readings (12 per day for that hour). At a given hour, eligible
station-month-hour summaries receive equal weight. These summaries therefore
describe the observed station network; they do not make its changing
composition equivalent to a fixed network.

A complete day has exactly 288 retained five-minute readings. Supplementary
complete-day estimates first average complete daily totals within each
station-month, then give each contributing station-month equal weight. The
complete-day fraction uses all expected station-days for the station IDs seen
anywhere in 2017--2024 as its denominator, so it reflects both recording
completeness and changes in station availability.

\section{Task 2: Thirty-minute exceedance probability}
\subsection{Target and features}
Each candidate is an observed station-time pair with a valid current rainfall
reading. Let $r(s,u)$ denote the rainfall observed at station $s$ at time $u$.
For threshold $\tau$, the 30-minute accumulation and exceedance label are
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    F_{30}(s,t)
    &= \sum_{j=1}^{6} r(s,t+5j\,\mathrm{min}),\\
    y(s,t,\tau)
    &= \mathbf{1}\left[
      F_{30}(s,t) > \tau
    \right].
  \end{aligned}
\]
The sum contains exactly six future observations, at $t+5$, $t+10$, $t+15$,
$t+20$, $t+25$ and $t+30$ minutes for the same station. The current reading is
not part of the target. All six observations must be present at their exact
five-minute timestamps; a sample missing any future observation is excluded,
and missing rainfall is not replaced by zero. An observed zero contributes
zero, and equality to $\tau$ is a non-exceedance. Forecasts without a current
observation are outside the prediction task.

The selected history model has 36 numerical predictor columns for cyclic time,
threshold and current or earlier same-station rainfall. All predictor
information is anchored at or before $t$; the six future readings define only
the outcome. The archive does not show when revised readings became available,
so this is a retrospective event-time analysis, not a verified real-time
forecast. Station ID is treated as categorical: StringIndexer and OneHotEncoder
keep invalid values, and OneHotEncoder does not drop the final category
(\texttt{dropLast=False}). VectorAssembler joins the station vector with the 36
numerical columns~\cite{mllib}. An unseen station maps to a retained category,
but the model has no training examples from which to learn its station-specific
effect. Calendar features and available rainfall history still contribute; the
time-based holdout does not estimate transfer to unseen stations. Logistic
regression uses standardized inputs; random forest and gradient-boosted trees
use the assembled inputs without standardization. Coordinates and future
rainfall are excluded from the predictors.

\subsection{Temporal partitions and training}
Split assignment is based on prediction time. Samples whose 30-minute target
reaches or crosses a partition boundary are excluded, so every retained target
window lies wholly within its assigned partition. Each valid prediction time
is expanded into six examples, one per configured rainfall threshold.

Using only 2017--2022, the climatology baseline estimates strict exceedance
rates by station, month, hour and threshold. It shrinks each station-month-hour
rate toward its month-hour rate, then the overall rate, using 100 windows per
step. Cells have no minimum support: unseen station-month-hour combinations
use the month-hour rate, and unseen month-hour combinations use the overall
rate. Thresholds with fewer than 100 positive or fewer than one negative
training window are excluded. No 2023 or 2024 outcomes enter the baseline.

ML candidates used a deterministic 2\% sample of valid training times; the
baseline used all valid training times. The selected GBT-B used depth 4 and 40
iterations. July--December 2023 was used to fit its isotonic calibration, and
2024 was reserved for final evaluation.

\subsection{Model selection}
Mean PR-AUC across the six primary thresholds ranked the candidates, with mean
Brier score as a tie-breaker. LR and GBT were assessed with feature sets A and
B; RF used B. Adding rainfall history increased mean selection PR-AUC from
0.0488 to 0.4852 for LR and from 0.0573 to 0.5266 for GBT. GBT-B ranked above
RF-B and LR-B among the history-feature candidates.

\subsection{Feature-family explanation}
The selected Spark GBT's normalized split importance was grouped using the
fitted feature-vector layout~\cite{gbtimportance}. The 90 station-vector
indices were combined as station identity; the remaining 36 numeric inputs
were mapped in assembler order to the feature families in
Figure~\ref{fig:feature-family-importance}. Current rainfall contributed
45.0\% and threshold 23.4\%. No split used station identity or window-support
features in this fitted model. This importance is predictive, not causal:
correlated lag and rolling features can redistribute importance among
themselves, and zero station importance here is not a station-ablation result.

At 0.5~mm, calibrated GBT-B increased PR-AUC from 0.0732 to 0.6345 and reduced
Brier score from 0.03436 to 0.01952; positive prevalence was 3.62\%. At the
fixed 0.5 probability cutoff, precision was 0.812, recall 0.471 and F1 0.596.
The baseline predicted no positives at this cutoff. Its precision and F1 are
undefined, and recall is zero. Across the six thresholds, the paired mean
Brier difference (GBT-B minus climatology) was $-0.00878$.

Across the six primary thresholds, holdout ECE was 0.00172 for calibrated
GBT-B and 0.00142 for the baseline. Threshold monotonicity violations affected
0.0121\% of primary-threshold predictions and 0.0151\% of intermediate-
threshold predictions; the largest adjacent upward probability difference
was 0.299. Thus, calibrated probabilities were not perfectly ordered across
thresholds.

Five intermediate thresholds on the same 2024 cohort had PR-AUC values from
0.3407 to 0.6568. They describe interpolation within the tested threshold

\section{Discussion and limitations}
No station met the 90\% annual-coverage rule in every year, so annual
differences describe a changing network and do not establish a climate trend.
Archived timestamps do not establish when revised readings became available,
so the results are not verified real-time forecasts.

The ML models used a 2\% training sample while the baseline used all valid
training times; the comparison therefore reflects different training-data
budgets.

\section{Conclusion}
Wet-reading frequency ranged from 1.94\% to 3.20\% across 2017--2024, and the
hourly peak occurred between 15:00 and 16:00 SGT in every year. For the 0.5-mm
Task 2 threshold, calibrated GBT-B achieved PR-AUC 0.6345 and Brier score
0.01952, compared with 0.0732 and 0.03436 for the climatology baseline. It had
higher PR-AUC and lower Brier score at each primary threshold. Run
\texttt{\runid} (commit \texttt{ea454b3}) produced these results; its input
manifest, configuration, notebooks and outputs are retained with the run.
Conclusions apply to retrospective records with complete future windows, not
to verified real-time availability or future station networks.
