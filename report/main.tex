\section*{Abstract}
This report analyses NEA five-minute rainfall records from 2017 to 2024. Wet-
reading frequency ranged from 1.94\% to 3.20\%; no station met the 90\% annual-
coverage rule in every year, so annual differences reflect a changing network.
For Task 2, five candidates were compared on a deterministic 10\% sample of
2022 after hyperparameter validation on 2021. GBT-B was selected and refitted
on all 34,541,470 valid 2017--2022 windows. A predetermined isotonic calibrator
used 10\% of 2023; equal-weight PAVA then made the six-threshold exceedance
curve non-increasing. On 6,374,476 held-out 2024 windows, final PAVA GBT-B
achieved PR-AUC 0.6345 and Brier score 0.01958 at 0.5~mm, compared with
0.0732 and 0.03436 for the climatology baseline. The final projection had zero
monotonicity violations across 31.87 million adjacent threshold pairs. The
2024 period had been observed in the historical v1 analysis, so it is reported
as a held-out evaluation period rather than an untouched blind test. Results
are retrospective and do not establish real-time data availability or future-
network performance.

\section{Introduction}
The report uses the National Environment Agency's Historical Rainfall across
Singapore collection, timestamped in Singapore Time. The catalog warns that
records may be missing and have not undergone climate-record quality control~\cite{nea}.

Task 1 describes all eight years. For Task 2, 2017--2020 supports hyperparameter
training, 2021 supports hyperparameter validation, 2022 is used for candidate
selection, the frozen winner is refitted on all valid 2017--2022 windows, 2023
is used for probability calibration, and 2024 is reserved for final evaluation.
Historical v1 metrics from 2024 were already observed and were not used to
choose any v2 model or prediction rule.

\section{Data and cleaning}
\subsection{Input and timestamp handling}
The frozen manifest records eight annual files, their schemas, row counts and
SHA-256 digests. They contain 48,538,713 rows and 12 columns. The rainfall field
is NEA reading type \texttt{TB1 Rainfall 5 Minute Total F}, in millimetres. The
download time was not recorded. The project's
interval-end convention treats a reading at time $t$ as rainfall accumulated
over $(t-5\text{ min},t]$; this is an analysis assumption, not an NEA-confirmed
timestamp definition.

Early 2017 records systematically end at second 59; later 2017 and 2018--2024
records end at second 00. The same \texttt{:59} convention appears in the
official 2016 dataset, which was not used here~\cite{nea}. In 2017, 3,480,779
timestamps fell on five-minute boundaries and 1,775,327 fell one second before
them. The early \texttt{:59} values were advanced one second for alignment, and
the source timestamps were retained. The publisher does not document the
convention change's meaning, so this is an alignment choice; other off-grid
offsets were rejected.

Validation covered parsing, station IDs, timestamps, nonnegative rainfall,
date/year, reading type and unit. Duplicate station-time keys were resolved
deterministically. Coordinates were used only for the supplementary spatial
summary. Missing readings remain gaps and were not filled with zero.

\subsection{Coverage definitions}
Coverage is the count of retained five-minute readings divided by the expected
slots (288 per calendar day) in the full calendar period, including periods
with no records. Rainfall
occurrence is the share of retained readings greater than zero; missing
readings are excluded from this denominator and are not treated as dry.
Conditional wet intensity is the mean five-minute rainfall total among
positive readings. A
station-month rainfall total is the sum of observed readings and is not
adjusted for missing slots.

Monthly summaries include station-months with at least 95\% coverage and give
each eligible station-month equal weight. For hourly summaries, the same
monthly rule applies, and each station-month-hour must also have at least 95\%
of its expected readings (12 per day for that hour). At a given hour, eligible
station-month-hour summaries receive equal weight. These summaries therefore
describe the observed station network; they do not make its changing
composition equivalent to a fixed network.

A complete day has exactly 288 retained five-minute readings. Supplementary
complete-day estimates first average complete daily totals within each
station-month, then give each contributing station-month equal weight. The
complete-day fraction uses all expected station-days for the station IDs seen
anywhere in 2017--2024 as its denominator, so it reflects both recording
completeness and changes in station availability.

\subsection{Computing Environment and Distributed Execution}
Run \texttt{\runid} used Google Cloud Dataproc in
\texttt{asia-southeast1}. The cluster had one \texttt{e2-standard-4} master
and eight \texttt{e2-highmem-2} workers, each with a 40~GB standard persistent
disk. Spark ran eight two-core executors with dynamic allocation disabled.
The configured Dataproc image was \texttt{2.2.87-debian12} with Spark 3.5.3;
the submitted jobs explicitly selected the Dataproc Conda Python interpreter
\texttt{/opt/conda/default/bin/python}. The fixed configuration provided the
parallel resources for full-data model fitting; no scaling study was run, so
these results do not estimate speedup or linear scalability.

\section{Task 2: Thirty-minute exceedance probability}
\subsection{Target and features}
Each candidate is an observed station-time pair with a valid current rainfall
reading. Let $r(s,u)$ denote rainfall observed at station $s$ at time $u$.
For threshold $\tau$, the 30-minute accumulation and exceedance label are
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    F_{30}(s,t)
    &= \sum_{j=1}^{6} r(s,t+5j\,\mathrm{min}),\\
    y(s,t,\tau)
    &= \mathbf{1}\left[F_{30}(s,t) > \tau\right].
  \end{aligned}
\]
The sum contains exactly six future observations at $t+5$ through $t+30$
minutes for the same station. All six must exist at their exact five-minute
timestamps; a sample missing any future observation is excluded, and missing
rainfall is not replaced by zero. The current reading is not part of the
target. Equality to $\tau$ is a non-exceedance.

The selected history feature set adds current and earlier same-station rainfall
to station identity, cyclic time and the rainfall threshold. All predictor
information is anchored at or before $t$; future readings define only the
outcome. The archive does not show when revised readings became available, so
this is a retrospective event-time analysis rather than a verified real-time
forecast. Station ID is categorical; unseen-station transfer is not estimated
by this time-based holdout. Coordinates and future rainfall are excluded from
the predictors. Logistic regression uses standardized inputs; random forest
and gradient-boosted trees use the assembled inputs without standardization
~\cite{mllib}.

\subsection{Final fitting, calibration and monotone prediction}
After selection, a new GBT-B model was fitted on all 34,541,470 valid
2017--2022 base windows (207,248,820 threshold-expanded rows). It was not
trained on a deterministic 2\% sample. The predetermined isotonic calibrator
was fitted on a deterministic 10\% sample of the 2023 calibration partition:
671,768 of 6,719,274 base windows, expanded to 4,030,608 threshold examples.
The calibration-sample ECE diagnostic fell from 0.02264 before calibration to
0.00165 after calibration. This is an in-sample calibration diagnostic, not
an independent estimate of calibration performance.

The final predictor applies equal-weight pool-adjacent-violators algorithm
(PAVA) to the six calibrated anchor probabilities
$[0.2,0.5,1,2,5,10]$~mm. The projection minimizes squared displacement subject
to a non-increasing probability sequence. Any requested threshold inside
$[0.2,10]$~mm is estimated by linear interpolation between projected anchors;
out-of-domain thresholds are rejected.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
No station met the 90\% annual-coverage rule in every year, so annual
differences describe a changing network and do not establish a climate trend.
Archived timestamps do not establish when revised readings became available,
so the results are not verified real-time forecasts. The selected 2022 score
is subject to model-selection optimism, and 2024 was previously observed in the
historical v1 analysis; it is a held-out evaluation period, not an untouched
blind test.

at the displayed precision and Brier changes from 0.01952 to 0.01958. At
10~mm, v2 PR-AUC is lower by 0.0073, while Brier changes by only 0.00002.
With the same fixed 0.5 probability cutoff at 0.5~mm, v2 recall rises from
0.471 to 0.527 and precision falls from 0.812 to 0.742; F1 rises from 0.596
to 0.616. The cutoff was not tuned in either run.

The principal v2 change is the training and probability protocol. V1 trained
its ML models on a deterministic 2\% sample and used January--June 2023 for
both pilot tuning and candidate comparison, then calibrated on July--December.
V2 refit the winner on all valid 2017--2022 windows, validated hyperparameters
on 2021, selected on a 10\% sample of 2022, and calibrated on a 10\% sample of
2023. V1's calibrated predictor had 0.0121\% primary-threshold and 0.0151\%
intermediate-threshold violations, with a largest adjacent increase of 0.299.
V2's final PAVA predictor has zero violations on the anchor curve and on the
five in-domain interpolated thresholds. Isotonic calibration alone still
leaves threshold violations, so the ordering guarantee comes from PAVA.

Holdout weighted ECE changed from 0.00172 in v1 to 0.001670 in v2; both are
slightly above their climatology baselines (0.00142 and 0.001425). The results
therefore support a stronger monotonicity guarantee and a cleaner temporal
protocol, while showing similar predictive performance rather than a broad
metric improvement.

The final supervised model used all valid 2017--2022 windows, while the
development comparison and calibration fit used their prespecified 10\%
samples. The requested 2/10/50/100\% scaling study was not run, so this report
does not make a scaling claim. The 2024 weighted ECE for the final model was
slightly higher than the climatology baseline even though its PR-AUC and Brier
score improved at all six thresholds. Predictions also require a valid current
observation, and labels require all six future readings.

\section{Conclusion}
Protocol v2 separated hyperparameter validation on 2021, model selection on
2022, full-data refitting on 2017--2022, calibration on 2023 and held-out
evaluation on 2024. GBT-B was selected from five candidates and fitted on
34,541,470 valid training windows. The final calibrated and PAVA-projected
predictor achieved zero threshold-monotonicity violations across 31.87 million
pairs. At 0.5~mm, its 2024 PR-AUC was 0.6345 and Brier score was 0.01958,
compared with 0.0732 and 0.03436 for climatology. These retrospective results
are similar to the rounded v1 metrics on the shared holdout; the v2 PR-AUC at
10~mm is lower. The clearest gain is exact monotonicity after PAVA, not a broad
increase in predictive scores. These retrospective results do not establish
real-time availability or performance on future station networks.
