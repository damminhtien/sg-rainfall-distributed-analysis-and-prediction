\section*{Abstract}
This project analyses eight annual files of Singapore five-minute rainfall
observations from 2017 to 2024 using Apache Spark. Task 1 describes within-year
and across-year patterns with coverage-based denominators. Wet-reading
frequency ranged from 1.94\% in 2019 to 3.20\% in 2023; among station-months
with at least 95\% observation coverage, mean observed monthly rainfall ranged
from 31.97 to 419.07~mm. No station met the 90\% annual-coverage rule in all
eight years, limiting fixed-network trend comparisons. Task 2 predicts whether
the next six complete readings exceed a rainfall threshold. A gradient-boosted
tree with same-station history features was selected on the first half of 2023
and calibrated on the second half. On the 2024 holdout, its calibrated
0.5-mm result had PR-AUC 0.6345 and Brier score 0.01952, compared with 0.0732
and 0.03436 for the climatology baseline, on 6,374,476 valid prediction times.
Model fitting used a deterministic 2\% sample of valid training times, while
the baseline used all valid training times. Results describe retrospective
prediction at observed timestamps; they do not establish real-time
availability or generalisation to future sensor deployments.

\section{Introduction}
The assignment asks for rainfall-pattern analysis within and across years and
a Spark MLlib model for short-horizon rainfall prediction. This report follows
those two tasks. It uses the National Environment Agency's Historical Rainfall
across Singapore collection, whose records are timestamped in Singapore Time.
The catalog warns that records may be missing and have not undergone the
quality-control procedures used for climate records~\cite{nea}.

The analysis processes one annual CSV for each year from 2017 through 2024.
Task 1 uses all eight years for descriptive summaries. Task 2 uses calendar
splits: 2017--2022 for training, January--June 2023 for model and parameter
selection, July--December 2023 for optional calibration, and 2024 for the final
holdout. Descriptive 2024 results from Task 1 were not used to select Task 2
models.

\section{Data and cleaning}
\subsection{Input and timestamp handling}
The frozen manifest lists eight files, their sizes, schemas, row counts and
SHA-256 digests. The files contain 48,538,713 rows and 12 columns. The rainfall
field is the NEA reading type \texttt{TB1 Rainfall 5 Minute Total F}, in
millimetres. The original download time was not recorded. The project's
interval-end convention treats a reading at time $t$ as rainfall accumulated
over $(t-5\text{ min},t]$; this is an analysis assumption, not an NEA-confirmed
timestamp definition.

Before cleaning, the notebook displayed the timestamp-offset distribution.
All 2018--2024 records had zero seconds modulo 300. In 2017, 3,480,779 records
had offset 0 and 1,775,327 had offset 299. The offset-299 records occur in the
early 2017 timestamp block; later 2017 records use offset 0. For the analytical
epoch only, the run advances those 1,775,327 records by one second. The raw
timestamp remains in the cleaned output. This correction aligns the early
block to the observed five-minute boundary but is not documented by the
dataset publisher, so it remains a limitation of the time interpretation.
Other off-grid offsets were not accepted.

Rows were checked for parse errors, missing station IDs, invalid timestamps,
invalid rainfall, negative rainfall, date/year mismatches, reading type and
unit. Duplicate station-time keys were resolved deterministically. Coordinates
are not required for the rainfall modelling data; they are filtered only for
the supplementary spatial summary. Missing readings remain gaps and are never
filled with zero.

\subsection{Coverage definitions}
Coverage is the count of retained five-minute readings divided by the expected
slots in the full station calendar period. For the main monthly summaries, a
station-month is eligible only when coverage is at least 95\%. Hourly wet
frequency further requires at least 95\% coverage within each station-hour,
and each eligible station-month contributes one value to the hourly average.
These rules reduce the effect of incomplete records but do not make the
changing station network equivalent to a fixed network.

\section{Task 2: Thirty-minute exceedance probability}
\subsection{Target and features}
Each candidate is an observed station-time pair with a valid current rainfall
reading. Let $r(s,u)$ denote the rainfall observed at station $s$ at time $u$.
For threshold $\tau$, the 30-minute accumulation and exceedance label are
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    F_{30}(s,t)
    &= \sum_{j=1}^{6} r(s,t+5j\,\mathrm{min}),\\
    y(s,t,\tau)
    &= \mathbf{1}\left[
      F_{30}(s,t) > \tau
    \right].
  \end{aligned}
\]
The sum contains exactly six future observations, at $t+5$, $t+10$, $t+15$,
$t+20$, $t+25$ and $t+30$ minutes for the same station. The current reading is
not part of the target. All six observations must be present at their exact
five-minute timestamps; a sample missing any future observation is excluded,
and missing rainfall is not replaced by zero. An observed zero contributes
zero, and equality to $\tau$ is a non-exceedance. Forecasts without a current
observation are outside the prediction task.

The selected history model uses 36 numerical predictors describing cyclic
calendar time, the threshold and current and earlier same-station rainfall.
The six future readings define only the outcome; predictor rainfall and
calendar values are anchored at or before $t$. Current rainfall is measured at
$t$, while the history summaries look backward. The archived data do not
establish when each value first became available, so this is an event-time
retrospective analysis rather than a verified real-time forecast. Station
identity is a separate categorical predictor, indexed and one-hot encoded with
Spark MLlib. The resulting station indicators are combined with the 36
numerical predictors to form the model input~\cite{mllib}.
Logistic regression uses standardized inputs; random forest and
gradient-boosted trees use the same inputs without standardization.

An unseen station is assigned to a reserved category that remains represented
in the encoded predictors. Because this category has no training observations,
the model cannot learn a station-specific effect for it. Calendar predictors
and any available same-station rainfall history remain usable, but the
time-based holdout does not measure performance at stations absent from
training. Station coordinates and future rainfall are excluded from the
predictors.

Split assignment is based on prediction time. Samples whose 30-minute target
reaches or crosses a partition boundary are excluded, so every retained target
window lies wholly within its assigned partition. Each valid prediction time
is expanded into six examples, one per configured rainfall threshold.

The climatology baseline uses outcomes from valid 2017--2022 training windows
only. It estimates station-month-hour exceedance probabilities with smoothed
broader-level fallbacks and serves as a comparison rather than an ML feature.
MLlib models use a deterministic 2\% sample of valid training
windows: 690,699 base times before threshold expansion. The pilot used 173,478
base times (approximately 0.5\%). Parameter pilots compared four logistic
regression settings, three random-forest settings and four gradient-boosted
tree settings. The best pilot settings were LR regularisation 0.01, RF with 50
trees and depth 8, and GBT with depth 4 and 40 iterations.

Jan--Jun 2023 was used both to select pilot settings and to compare the final
candidates. The selection scores are therefore part of the model-selection
process, not an independent estimate of generalisation. The 2024 partition
was reserved for the final comparison.

\subsection{Model selection}
Selection used mean PR-AUC across the six supported thresholds as the primary
criterion, with mean Brier score as the tie-breaker within configured
tolerances. LR and GBT were each assessed with the base and history feature
sets; RF used the history feature set. Adding same-station history increased
the mean selection PR-AUC from 0.0488 to 0.4852 for LR and from 0.0573 to
0.5266 for GBT. Among the common history-feature candidates, GBT-B ranked
above RF-B and LR-B.

At the 0.5-mm threshold, calibrated GBT-B increased PR-AUC from 0.0732 to
0.6345 and reduced Brier score from 0.03436 to 0.01952. The positive fraction
was 3.62\%, so PR-AUC is more informative than accuracy for this imbalanced
target. Across all threshold rows, the paired mean Brier difference
(GBT-B minus climatology) was $-0.00878$. A bootstrap over 53 calendar-week
blocks gave a 95\% interval from $-0.01089$ to $-0.00672$ using 1,000
replicates. This interval describes the pooled threshold-expanded losses under
the weekly resampling procedure.

On the 2024 holdout, ECE was 0.00172 for calibrated GBT-B and 0.00142 for the
baseline. Calibration improved the selected model's probability reliability
relative to its selection-period trigger, but its held-out ECE was slightly
higher than the baseline. The observed monotonicity violation rate across the
six primary thresholds was 0.0121\%; across the five intermediate thresholds
it was 0.0151\%. The largest upward probability increase between adjacent
thresholds was 0.299. The calibrated probabilities are therefore not
perfectly ordered for every threshold pair.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
The descriptive summaries show a pronounced afternoon increase in wet-reading
frequency and substantial variation across months and years. The 95\%
coverage rule limits the influence of partial station-months, but eligibility
still varies by month. The absence of a common station cohort across all eight
years prevents a direct fixed-network annual comparison. Annual differences
should be read as differences in the available records, not as a climate trend.

The selected history-feature GBT improved PR-AUC and Brier score over the
climatology baseline on the 2024 observed-station/time cohort. The evaluation
does not establish transfer to stations absent from training or to future
operational conditions. The archive is retrospective and contains update
timestamps; this project does not reconstruct when revised readings first
became available. It assumes instantaneous availability at the recorded event
time. Missing current readings are outside the prediction contract.

Supervised ML fitting used a 2\% deterministic sample, while the climatology
baseline used the complete valid training partition. Although both models were
evaluated on identical 2024 rows, this training-budget difference is part of
the comparison. Parameter tuning and final selection both used January--June
2023, so the selection metrics are optimistic for model choice. The 2024
holdout remains untouched by post-test tuning. Finally, the one-second 2017
epoch correction affects the interpretation of those early timestamps and
should be revisited if authoritative timestamp semantics become available.
